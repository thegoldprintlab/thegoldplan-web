A trading journal built for one instrument, not thirty

Gold is not forex with a different ticker. It moves on real yields, on central-bank headlines, and on a spread that widens exactly when the move starts. A journal that treats XAUUSD as one row among forty pairs will average away the only patterns that matter.

The four scoreboards

  • Setup — which of your named setups actually pays.
  • Session — Asia, London, overlap, New York. Where your edge lives.
  • Account — ROI per account, including prop vs personal separately.
  • Emotion — calm, hesitant, FOMO, revenge, and what each one costs.

Numbers on the dashboard

Total net P&L, win rate, profit factor, trades won/lost, average win, average loss, best trade, worst trade, and a daily equity curve. All of it filters by account, because "all accounts" is a comforting view that hides a blown challenge.

How I use it

I trade gold on a prop account and on my own. Before the London open I look at one number: how much of today's limit is left. After the session I log the trades I took and, more usefully, note the ones I wanted to take and did not. Over 1,517 logged trades across three accounts, the pattern that cost the most was never a bad entry — it was the second and third entry after a loss.

Who this is not for

  • If you want signals, this is not that.
  • If you want an EA that trades for you, this is not that either.
  • If you trade twelve instruments and want one blended report, a general journal will serve you better than a gold-specific one.

If you trade one instrument, seriously, the journal should be shaped like that instrument.
