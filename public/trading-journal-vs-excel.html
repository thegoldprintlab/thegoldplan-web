Trading journal vs Excel: where the spreadsheet actually fails

If your spreadsheet is already keeping you disciplined, keep it. This page is not going to pretend a paid journal beats a free file for everyone.

It is going to be specific about the four things a spreadsheet does not do, and let you decide whether any of them cost you money.

1. A spreadsheet cannot warn you during the session

A sheet tells you what happened. By the time you open it, the third trade is already filled. The one job a journal can do that a sheet cannot is interrupt you at 80% of your daily limit — the moment before the account-ending trade, not the evening after it.

2. A spreadsheet cannot score by emotion

You can add an "emotion" column in Excel in about ten seconds. What you cannot do cheaply is the pivot that answers: what is my net P&L when the emotion is revenge compared with calm, and is that gap bigger than my entire monthly profit? That pivot is the product.

3. A spreadsheet cannot import an MT5 report

Exporting MT5 to Excel and then hand-mapping the columns into your own sheet works exactly once. After that, the format drifts, a column moves, and you stop doing it. Import is the difference between a journal you keep and a journal you abandon in week three.

4. A spreadsheet does not survive a phone

Most gold trades are logged on a phone, minutes after the fact, not at a desk. Excel on a phone is a pinch-and-zoom exercise. The Quick Log API exists because one tap on a Shortcut beats opening a spreadsheet.
