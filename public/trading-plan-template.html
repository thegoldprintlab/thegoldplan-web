XAUUSD trading plan template: one page, written before the session

A trading plan is not a document about trading. It is a set of decisions made while you were calm, so the version of you at 08:00 UTC with a live chart does not have to make any. This page gives you the template and explains what each field is for.

It is one page on purpose. If it does not fit on a sheet, you will not read it before the London open. Fill it once per session, in writing, before the first candle of your session closes.

The template

XAUUSD session plan

Date / session: ____ (Sydney / Tokyo / London / New York) Bias: long / short / none Bias source: ____ (HTF level, news, structure) Setup name: ____ (one setup only, name it exactly as you wrote it in your journal) Entry trigger: ____ (a price level or a confirmed pattern, not "when it looks right") Stop: ____ (price, not pips: $____ move = $____ risk per 1.0 lot) Target: ____ (price) Invalidation: ____ (what kills the idea) Risk: ____% of $____ = $____ Lot size: ____ (round DOWN to broker step) Max trades this session: ____ Done for the day when: ____

Copy it into a note, a paper sheet, or the notes field of your journal. The medium does not matter. Written before the session is the only rule.

Why each field exists

Bias and bias source

"Long" or "short" with no source is a mood, not a bias. The source field forces you to name the evidence: a higher-timeframe level, the London open direction, or "none". Trading with no bias is a valid plan. Most losing days on gold start with a bias that was never written down.

Setup name and entry trigger

One setup per session. The name should match a setup you have already logged and reviewed, so the win rate you are counting on is your own measured one. The trigger is a price level or a confirmed pattern. "When it looks right" is how a reversal candle at London open becomes three revenge trades.

Stop in price, then convert

Write the stop as a price first (2,388.00, not "30 pips"), because the chart gives you prices and your risk math needs the distance. On XAUUSD a $1.00 price move is $100 per 1.0 lot, so a $3.00 stop is $30 per lot. If your risk budget is $20, the plan allows 0.66 lots, rounded down to the broker step. The lot size calculator does this conversion.

Target and invalidation

Target is where you take profit. Invalidation is the condition that proves the idea wrong and gets you flat even if the stop has not been hit yet: a close above a level, a news print, the session ending. Plans without invalidation hold losing trades to the stop "because it has not hit yet".

Risk, lot size, and the two caps

Risk is a percent of the account and a dollar number. Both, always, so a bad week is visible in dollars. The max-trades cap and the "done when" line are the two fields that actually save accounts on gold: the day you break them is the day the account ends, and the template makes the cap exist before the session does.

A filled example

Example (numbers, not advice)

Date / session: Tue, London Bias: short Bias source: daily supply 2,384-2,387, lower high on H4 Setup name: supply retest rejection Entry trigger: 2,385.00 limit, cancel if not filled in 90 min Stop: 2,388.00 ($3.00 move = $30 per 1.0 lot) Target: 2,379.00 ($6.00 move, 2R) Invalidation: H1 close above 2,388.00 Risk: 1% of $2,000 = $20 Lot size: 0.66 (round down 0.66) Max trades this session: 2 Done for the day when: +2R or 2 losses
The arithmetic: $20 risk at $30 per lot allows 0.66 lots. A $6.00 target move returns $60 per lot, about $39.60 at 0.66 lots, which is the 2R. If the account were $500, the same stop would allow 0.16 lots, and the same trade is still 1% risk. That is the whole point of writing the stop in price first: the size adapts, the risk does not.

The pre-entry checklist

  • Is the bias written down, with a source?
  • Is the entry trigger a price level, not a feeling?
  • Is the stop a price, and is the lot size derived from it (rounded down)?
  • Is the risk at most 1% of the account for this single trade?
  • Is the invalidation a condition I will actually act on, not "let me see"?
  • Is the max-trades cap for this session written and visible?
  • Did I check the calendar for news inside my session window?

If any line is blank, you do not have a plan. You have a chart open.

What to do with the plan after the session

The plan is half the loop. The other half is the journal entry: did you follow your own sheet, and what did the outcome teach you about that setup? A plan followed and lost beats a plan abandoned and won, because the first one produces data. The journal keeps the outcome next to the plan, so the template gets better every week instead of getting rewritten every morning.
