The emotion column nobody fills in, and why it is the one that pays

Almost every trading journal template has an emotion field. Almost nobody fills it in, because "how did you feel" is a bad question. It produces a word, and a word does not aggregate.

Ask a narrower question

Instead of "how do you feel", pick from a fixed short list. Fixed options are what make the scoreboard possible:

LabelThe trade it usually produces
CalmSetup was there, size was normal, stop was honoured.
HesitantEntry was late, size was small, the move happened without you.
FOMOEntry was after the move, stop was wide or absent.
RevengeEntry came within minutes of a loss, size was larger.
TiredEntry in a session you do not normally trade.

Five labels is enough. Twenty labels is a taxonomy nobody maintains.

What the chart actually tells you

Once trades carry a label, one comparison becomes possible: net P&L per label. In most journals the result is lopsided in a way the trader already suspected but never priced. Revenge trades are rarely the most numerous. They are almost always the most expensive per trade.

That single number does something a rule cannot: it makes the cost of the next revenge entry concrete. "I should not do this" is a feeling. "This label has cost me $840 this quarter" is a fact.

Two fields, not one

Emotion before the entry and emotion after the exit are different things, and they diverge in the trades that matter. Calm going in, angry coming out is a normal stop-out. Angry going in, calm coming out is the one that ends accounts.

How to start without changing your process

  1. Tag the last thirty trades you already have, from memory. Memory is fine for a month.
  2. Look at the net P&L per label. Do not fix anything yet.
  3. For two weeks, tag every new trade at entry. One tap, chosen from the fixed list.
  4. Compare the two periods. If the expensive label has not shrunk, the tagging is not doing the work — the rule is missing.

The honest limit

Emotion tags are self-reported and they are not science. Nobody is measuring your cortisol. What they are is a cheap proxy that turns a vague pattern into a number you can compare month to month, and that is enough to change behaviour.
